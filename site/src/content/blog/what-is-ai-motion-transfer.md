**AI motion transfer** takes the movement from one video and applies it to someone or something else. It copies the dance, the sprint or the camera move, then recasts it with a new face, outfit or character, and the timing stays the same as the original.

It's the technique behind most "put me in this trend" videos you see on TikTok and Reels.

## How it works

Motion transfer has three inputs:

1. **A source video.** This is the motion you want: a dance, a sports move or a walk into the frame.
2. **A subject.** One or more reference images of the person, character or product that should do the move.
3. **A prompt** (optional). Use it for the setting, style or anything else you want to change.

The model reads the source frame by frame, including body pose, camera path and timing. Then it generates a new video where your subject performs that motion. A good model keeps the subject's **identity locked**, so the face and outfit look the same from the first frame to the last instead of drifting.

## What creators use it for

- **Joining trends** without learning the choreography.
- **Virtual creators and mascots** who need the same look in every post.
- **Product ads** where a product swap keeps the hands and lighting from a clip that already performs.
- **Previs for film** to block out a scene with stand-in footage, then recast it.

## Getting clean results

- **Pick clear source footage.** One subject, good light and not too much motion blur.
- **Use strong references.** A sharp, front-facing photo works best. Add a full-body shot and an outfit detail to lock more of the look.
- **Match the framing.** A full-body dance needs a full-body reference, not just a headshot.
- **Start short.** Five to ten seconds is fast to render and easy to iterate on. Go longer once you're happy with the look.
